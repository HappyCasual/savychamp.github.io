How to Start Making Your Own Mobile Games (A Guide for Beginners in India)

Published on: April 30, 2026 | By: SavyChamp Developer

If you are a student in college or someone working in a regular IT job in India, you might have thought at least once: "I play so many games every day, what if I create my own game?" Making a game and putting it on the Google Play Store for the world to see is a very proud feeling. But most people drop the idea thinking that game development is too difficult or requires a lot of money.

As the solo developer behind SavyChamp, let me tell you a secret: Making simple, fun games is easier today than it has ever been. You don't need a huge team, you don't need a supercomputer, and you definitely don't need lakhs of rupees. Here is a simple, step-by-step guide to help you start your game development journey right from your bedroom.

Step 1: Don't Start With a Big Idea (Keep it Simple!)

The biggest mistake beginners make is dreaming too big. They think, "I will make the next PUBG or GTA." Please, stop right there. Big 3D games take years to make and require big teams. When you are just starting, your goal should be to learn, not to make a blockbuster on day one.

Start with a simple 2D game. Think of ideas like a ball bouncing, matching colors, or arranging puzzle pieces. For example, my game Emoji Sentence is not a massive 3D adventure. It is a simple, logic-based game where you guess words using emojis. It is fun, easy to code, and people love it. Start small, finish it, and gain confidence.

Step 2: Choose the Right Free Tool (Game Engine)

You don't need to write thousands of lines of raw code from scratch. We have software called "Game Engines" that do the heavy lifting for you. In India, most indie developers use these two popular (and free!) engines:

  • Unity: This is the most famous game engine. If you want to make 2D or 3D games for Android or iOS, Unity is fantastic. The language you need to learn for Unity is C# (C-Sharp). It is very easy to pick up if you know basic coding.
  • Godot: If your laptop is old and runs slowly, Godot is a lifesaver. It is very lightweight, 100% free, and perfect for 2D games. It uses its own language called GDScript, which is very similar to Python.

Step 3: YouTube is Your Best Teacher

You do not need to pay thousands of rupees for online courses. In India, we have access to the biggest free university in the world: YouTube. There are countless channels, both in English and Hindi, that teach game development from zero to hero.

Search for things like "Unity beginner tutorial 2D" or "How to make a Flappy Bird clone in Godot." Follow along step-by-step. Don't just watch the video like a movie; open your software and copy what they are doing. Making mistakes and fixing errors is how you actually learn.

Step 4: Create Simple Art and Sounds (Jugaad is Okay!)

You might think, "I don't know how to draw, how will my game look good?" You don't need to be an artist. There are websites like Kenney.nl where you can download thousands of free, high-quality game assets (characters, buttons, backgrounds) legally.

For my game Tamcall, I focused more on the logic and the user experience rather than complex graphics. A clean, simple UI (User Interface) often looks much better than messy, over-designed graphics. For sounds, websites like Freesound.org have great free sound effects.

Step 5: Learn to Accept Bugs and Errors

Coding a game is 20% writing code and 80% finding out why that code is not working. You will face errors. Your character might fly off the screen, or the game might crash. Do not panic. Every developer in the world faces this. Just copy the error message, paste it into Google or ask an AI, and you will find the solution. Patience is the most important skill for a game developer.

Conclusion

Making a game is a beautiful process. It mixes logic, art, sound, and psychology all into one package. The day you finally export your game to your Android phone, install the APK, and show it to your friends or parents—that feeling is priceless.

So, don't wait for the "perfect time." Download Unity or Godot today, watch a simple tutorial, and make a square jump on your screen. That is your first step. Keep practicing, keep building, and who knows, maybe your next small idea will become the next big hit. Welcome to the world of game development!
